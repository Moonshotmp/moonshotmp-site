WHY A CHRONIC TENDON STOPS HEALING, AND HOW SHOCKWAVE RESTARTS IT

Acute injuries heal because inflammation brings blood, growth factors, and repair cells to the site. A tendon that has hurt for six months has usually exited that phase. The tissue is degenerated, poorly vascularized, and the body has stopped treating it as a problem worth fixing.

Shockwave delivers controlled mechanical stress that the tissue reads as a fresh injury. That triggers new blood vessel growth, growth factor release, breakdown of calcified deposits, and a reset of local pain signaling. You are not masking the pain. You are giving the tendon a second chance to finish healing.

The technology started as a way to break kidney stones without surgery. Decades of orthopedic research later, ESWT is one of the most evidence-backed non-invasive options for chronic tendinopathy.

What the acoustic waves do

  • •Stimulate neovascularization, new blood vessels into avascular tendon
  • •Release growth factors (eNOS, VEGF, BMP) that drive repair
  • •Fragment calcium deposits so the body can reabsorb them
  • •Disrupt substance P pain signaling for lasting relief

WHO SHOULD NOT GET SHOCKWAVE

The intake form screens for each of these. If one applies, a provider will reach out before your visit.

  • •Pregnancy
  • •Blood clotting disorders or blood thinners (warfarin, Eliquis, Xarelto)
  • •Active infection, open wound, or rash at the treatment site
  • •Tumor or cancer in or near the area
  • •Open growth plates (under 18)
  • •Cortisone injection at the site within the last 6 weeks
  • •Recent fracture or surgery at the site (last 3 months)
  • •Pacemaker or implanted electronic device

AFTER YOUR SESSION

  • •Expect soreness for 1 to 2 days. That is the healing response you paid for.
  • •Skip anti-inflammatories for 48 hours. Ibuprofen and naproxen blunt the effect. Ice for comfort is fine.
  • •Light activity same day, no high-impact for 48 hours. Walk, cycle, swim. Save the box jumps.
  • •Do the loading exercises. Shockwave opens the window; the exercises are what rebuild the tendon.
  • •Give it 6 to 12 weeks. Some people feel better after session one. The tissue change is what lasts, and it takes time.

SHOCKWAVE THERAPY FAQ

How much does shockwave therapy cost in Park Ridge?

A single 30-minute treatment is $105. The 6-Treatment Recovery Program is $570 ($95 each) and the 8-Treatment Recovery Program is $720 ($90 each). Programs are prepaid and valid for 6 months. Self-pay; HSA and FSA cards accepted.

How many sessions will I need?

Most chronic tendon conditions respond in 3 to 6 sessions about a week apart. Calcific shoulder and long-standing patellar tendinopathy may need 6 to 8. Some patients feel a change after the first session; the full result develops over 6 to 12 weeks as the tissue remodels.

Does it hurt?

It feels like rapid, firm tapping over the sore spot. Uncomfortable but tolerable for most people, and the intensity is adjusted to you. Soreness for a day or two afterward is normal.

Do I need a referral or a consult first?

No referral. Book online at least 24 hours ahead, complete the intake, and a licensed provider reviews it before you arrive. If you are not a candidate, the session is refunded.

Is shockwave better than a cortisone shot?

Different jobs. Cortisone calms inflammation fast but does not heal the tendon, and repeated shots weaken it. Shockwave stimulates repair, so the improvement is slower and lasts. If you already had a cortisone shot and the pain came back, shockwave is usually the better next step, after a 6-week gap.

Does shockwave work for plantar fasciitis?

It is the most studied indication. Systematic reviews report meaningful pain reduction in roughly 65 to 80 percent of chronic heel-pain patients who did not respond to stretching, orthotics, or rest. Most orthopedic guidelines recommend it before surgery.

Do you take insurance?

Shockwave is self-pay at $105 per session. Most plans do not cover ESWT. HSA and FSA cards work, and we provide an itemized receipt on request.

Can I train the same day?

Light activity, yes. Avoid high-impact work on the treated area for 48 hours and skip ibuprofen or naproxen for the same window. Keep up the loading exercises your provider gives you.
