QUICK ANSWER: WHAT DOES PRP MICRONEEDLING COST?

$600-1,000

Per session (Chicago suburbs)

$1,800-2,700

3-session series (with package discount)

Cash-Pay Only

Not covered by insurance

PRP microneedling is a cash-pay procedure. No insurance carrier covers it — it's classified as cosmetic regardless of the indication. Plan on $600-1,000 per session in the Chicago suburbs, with most medical practices offering 10-15% package discounts for a 3-session series.

Most patients need 3-4 sessions spaced 4-6 weeks apart, plus 1-2 maintenance sessions per year after the initial series. So the realistic first-year investment is $2,400-3,700 — the initial series plus one maintenance treatment.

The range is wide because pricing depends on PRP preparation quality, provider credentials, treatment area, and market. The sections below break down exactly what drives that variation and what to look for.

HSA/FSA note: PRP microneedling is performed by a licensed medical provider, which makes it potentially eligible for HSA and FSA reimbursement. Using pre-tax dollars effectively cuts your out-of-pocket cost by 25-35%. Ask your HSA/FSA administrator about eligibility before your first session.

WHAT DRIVES THE PRICE

A $600 PRP microneedling session and a $1,000 session aren't necessarily the same treatment. Here's what accounts for the price difference:

PRP Preparation System

This is the biggest variable. FDA 510(k)-cleared PRP kits cost $75-150 per patient use. They produce validated, consistent platelet concentrations (3-5x baseline). Cheaper non-cleared systems cost $15-30 per kit but produce inconsistent PRP quality with lower platelet yields. The PRP system directly affects treatment efficacy — it's not a place to cut corners.

Provider Credentials

In Illinois, microneedling is classified as a medical procedure — it requires a licensed medical provider (physician, NP, PA). Physician-owned medical practices with board-certified providers charge more than med spas, but you're getting medical oversight, proper protocols, and accountability. An aesthetician cannot legally perform microneedling in IL, regardless of what some med spas advertise.

Treatment Area

Full face is the standard treatment area and the basis for most pricing. Adding the neck or decolletage increases the cost — more surface area means more PRP needed, more microneedling time, and potentially a larger blood draw. Targeted treatments (under-eye area only, acne scars only) may cost less than full face.

Geographic Market

PRP microneedling in Manhattan or Beverly Hills runs $1,200-2,000+ per session. Chicago suburbs like Park Ridge, Des Plaines, and Skokie typically fall in the $600-1,000 range. Rural markets may be lower. Real estate, staffing costs, and local demand all factor into pricing.

Why cheap PRP should raise red flags. If someone is advertising PRP microneedling for $200-300, ask what PRP system they use. Ask who performs the procedure. Ask if the system is FDA-cleared. The consumable cost alone (PRP kit + microneedling tips + numbing cream + supplies) is $100-200 per treatment. At $200 all-in, the math doesn't work unless something is being cut. PER-SESSION COST BREAKDOWN

When you pay $600-1,000 for a PRP microneedling session, here's where that money actually goes. Understanding the components helps you evaluate whether a provider's pricing is reasonable — or suspiciously low.

PRP Preparation Kit

FDA 510(k)-cleared, single-use kit with collection tube, separation gel, and transfer syringe. This is the core consumable that determines PRP quality.

$75-150

Microneedling Device Tips

Single-use, sterile needle cartridge for the microneedling pen. New tip for every patient — non-negotiable from an infection control standpoint.

$20-40

Blood Draw Supplies

Venipuncture kit, tubes, bandaging, gloves. Standard phlebotomy supplies.

$5-10

Topical Numbing Cream

Compounded lidocaine/prilocaine applied 30-45 minutes pre-procedure for patient comfort.

$10-20

Provider Time (60-90 min)

Licensed NP or PA performing the blood draw, PRP preparation, microneedling treatment, and post-care instructions. This is the majority of the cost.

$200-400

Facility Overhead

Treatment room, centrifuge equipment, sterilization, medical waste disposal, insurance, staffing, and regulatory compliance.

$150-300

Total consumable cost per treatment: $110-220. Add provider time and facility overhead, and you can see why legitimate PRP microneedling costs $600-1,000. There's no hidden markup conspiracy — it's a labor-intensive, consumable-heavy medical procedure. Practices advertising $150-250 PRP facials are cutting one or more of these line items.

PACKAGE PRICING: WHY IT MAKES SENSE

PRP microneedling is a cumulative treatment. One session produces modest improvement. The real results come from the compounding collagen remodeling across 3-4 sessions. This is why most practices offer package discounts — they want you to commit to enough sessions to actually see meaningful results.

Stopping after one session is like doing one week of physical therapy — you started the process but didn't give it enough time to produce meaningful structural change.

Ask your provider about package terms before committing. Reputable practices allow you to use package sessions over a reasonable timeframe (6-12 months) and don't penalize you if life gets in the way of your schedule.

WHAT TO WATCH OUT FOR

The PRP microneedling market has a discount problem. Low-cost providers attract patients with cheap pricing and deliver subpar results — which makes patients think PRP microneedling doesn't work. Here are the red flags:

Groupon/Deal-Site Pricing ($150-250)

The consumable cost alone for a legitimate PRP microneedling treatment is $110-220. At $150 all-in, the provider is losing money on supplies before paying for their time, facility, or overhead. Something is being cut: non-FDA-cleared PRP kits, diluted PRP (stretched with saline to treat multiple patients from one kit), reused microneedling tips, or the "PRP" isn't actually PRP at all. A $150 PRP facial is not the same treatment as a $750 one — it's a different procedure with the same name.

Non-FDA-Cleared PRP Systems

FDA 510(k) clearance means the PRP preparation system has been validated to produce consistent platelet concentrations. Non-cleared systems are cheaper ($15-30 per kit vs. $75-150) but produce variable PRP quality — you might get 2x platelet concentration one day and 5x the next. Consistency matters when you're trying to produce predictable clinical outcomes across a treatment series. Ask your provider which PRP system they use and whether it's FDA-cleared.

Aesthetician-Performed Procedures

In Illinois, microneedling is classified as a medical procedure. It must be performed by a licensed medical provider — physician, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant. An aesthetician cannot legally perform microneedling in IL, regardless of their training or the med spa's marketing. If the person performing your procedure doesn't have medical credentials, the practice is operating outside IL medical practice law. This isn't a technicality — it's a patient safety and legal compliance issue.

Bait-and-Switch Upselling

Some practices advertise a low base price for microneedling, then add PRP as an upsell once you're in the chair. Others quote a "starting at" price for a small treatment area (forehead only), then quote the full-face price during the consultation. Get the all-in price for your specific treatment plan in writing before scheduling. A reputable practice will give you a clear, complete quote without pressure.

Hidden Add-On Charges

Watch for separate charges for numbing cream, the blood draw, post-treatment serums, or "medical oversight fees" that aren't included in the quoted price. Your quoted price should include everything: numbing, blood draw, PRP preparation, microneedling, and post-treatment care. If the final bill is higher than the quote, that's a provider problem, not a you problem.

Questions to ask any provider before booking: What PRP preparation system do you use? Is it FDA 510(k)-cleared? Who performs the procedure (credentials)? Does the quoted price include everything (numbing, blood draw, PRP, microneedling)? How many sessions do you recommend and what's the package price? These questions separate legitimate practices from discount operators.

IS PRP MICRONEEDLING WORTH THE COST?

A 3-session PRP microneedling series is a $1,800-2,700 investment. Whether that's worth it depends on how you frame the math.

Cost Per Year of Results

Results from a 3-session series last 12-18 months. Annual maintenance (1-2 sessions) costs $600-1,000. So after the initial investment, you're looking at $50-85 per month to maintain the results. That's less than most premium skincare routines — and the results are structural, not surface-level.

Compared to Skincare Products

The average American woman spends $300-400 per year on skincare products. High-end retinol serums, vitamin C, peptide creams, and clinical-grade products can push that to $1,000-2,000+ per year. Those products work on the skin surface. PRP microneedling remodels collagen in the dermis. They're complementary, not competing — but per dollar of visible improvement, PRP delivers more structural change.

The Medical Premium

PRP microneedling at a medical practice costs more than a med spa. That premium pays for: a licensed medical provider performing the treatment, FDA-cleared PRP systems, proper sterile technique and infection control, medical oversight and accountability, and the ability to adjust needle depth and treatment parameters based on clinical assessment — not a one-size-fits-all protocol.

For Post-Weight-Loss Patients

Patients who've lost significant weight — especially on GLP-1 medications — often experience skin laxity that skincare products can't address. PRP microneedling stimulates collagen production in tissue that's lost structural support. The cost is often more palatable when framed against the alternative: surgical skin removal at $5,000-15,000+. Read our guide to PRP after weight loss.

The honest answer: PRP microneedling is a legitimate, evidence-backed treatment that produces real structural improvement in skin quality. It's not cheap, and it shouldn't be — the procedure involves your own blood products, medical-grade equipment, and a licensed provider's time. If your skin concerns are mild, regular microneedling or chemical peels may be sufficient at a lower price point. If you have moderate-to-significant scarring, texture issues, or post-weight-loss skin laxity, PRP microneedling delivers a level of improvement that cheaper alternatives can't match.

PRP MICRONEEDLING COST FAQ

Does insurance cover PRP microneedling?

No. PRP microneedling is classified as a cosmetic procedure and is not covered by any insurance carrier. This applies regardless of the indication — even if you're treating acne scarring, insurance considers microneedling cosmetic. However, you may be able to use HSA or FSA funds since PRP microneedling is performed by a licensed medical provider.

Can I use my HSA or FSA to pay for PRP microneedling?

In many cases, yes. PRP microneedling is a medical procedure performed by a licensed provider, which makes it potentially eligible for HSA and FSA reimbursement. Moonshot Medical provides itemized receipts with procedure codes for submission to your HSA/FSA administrator. Using pre-tax dollars effectively reduces your cost by 25-35% depending on your tax bracket — turning a $750 session into $490-560 after tax savings.

How many sessions do I need and what's the total cost?

Most patients need 3-4 sessions spaced 4-6 weeks apart for optimal results. Total cost for a 3-session series is typically $1,800-2,700 (less with package pricing). After the initial series, budget for 1-2 maintenance sessions per year at $600-1,000 each. First-year all-in cost: approximately $2,400-3,700. Subsequent years: $600-1,000 for maintenance.

Why is PRP microneedling more expensive than regular microneedling?

The PRP component adds $200-400 per session. That premium covers: the blood draw and venipuncture supplies, an FDA-cleared PRP preparation kit ($75-150 per use), centrifuge processing time, and additional provider time. You're getting concentrated growth factors from your own blood delivered directly into the dermis — not a generic hyaluronic acid serum. The clinical evidence supports better outcomes for the additional cost, particularly for acne scarring. Read our full PRP vs. microneedling comparison.

Do you offer payment plans or financing?

Moonshot Medical offers package pricing for 3-session and multi-session series at a 10-15% discount over single-session pricing. Third-party medical financing (CareCredit, Cherry) is also available with 0% APR promotional periods for qualifying patients. Ask about available options during your consultation — we'll help you find a payment structure that works.

Is a $150 PRP microneedling deal legitimate?

At that price, something is being cut. The PRP kit alone costs $75-150 wholesale for an FDA-cleared system. Add microneedling tips, numbing cream, blood draw supplies, provider time, and facility overhead — a legitimate treatment cannot be profitably delivered for $150. Common shortcuts: non-FDA-cleared PRP systems, diluted PRP, aesthetician-performed procedures (illegal for microneedling in IL), or bait-and-switch upselling once you arrive.

How long do results last?

Results from a full 3-4 session series typically last 12-18 months. The collagen remodeling is structural and durable — but it doesn't stop the aging process. Annual maintenance sessions (1-2 per year) sustain the improvements. Over 3 years, the total investment averages roughly $100-175 per month for sustained results. Compare that to $150-300/month in high-end skincare products that work only at the skin surface.

What's the total cost of a full treatment plan?

Initial 3-session series: $1,800-2,700 (less with package pricing). Year 1 total (series + 1 maintenance): $2,400-3,700. Year 2 and beyond (maintenance only): $600-1,000/year. Three-year total: approximately $3,600-5,700. That's $100-160 per month averaged over 3 years for structural skin rejuvenation that compounds over time.
