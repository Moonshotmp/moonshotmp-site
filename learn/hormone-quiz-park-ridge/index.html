WHY HORMONE SYMPTOMS TRAVEL TOGETHER

Hormonal decline doesn't cause one symptom. It causes patterns. Understanding your pattern is the first step toward fixing it.

Men: Testosterone Decline

Testosterone affects energy, cognition, mood, sleep, muscle, fat storage, and sexual function. When it declines — starting around age 30 at roughly 1% per year — multiple systems are affected simultaneously. That's why "tired, foggy, gaining weight, low drive" isn't four problems. It's one.

Common patterns: fatigue + recovery, brain fog + mood, body changes + physical symptoms.

If you're a man tracking energy, recovery, mood, or body-composition changes, our Hormone Health Quiz matches your symptom pattern to one of 10 archetypes.

Women: Estrogen, Progesterone & Testosterone

Women's hormone decline involves multiple hormones shifting at different rates. Progesterone often drops first (disrupting sleep and mood), followed by estrogen (hot flashes, brain fog, vaginal/bladder changes), and testosterone (energy, libido, muscle loss). The combination creates overlapping symptoms that get dismissed as "stress" or "aging."

Common patterns: sleep + mood, hot flashes, body + sexual + bladder changes.

If you're a woman tracking these symptoms, our Perimenopause Screener uses the validated 11-item Menopause Rating Scale plus a safety screen for hormone-therapy contraindications. It's purpose-built for the perimenopause/menopause transition — narrower scope, more clinically relevant output.

COMMON QUESTIONS

How do I know if my symptoms are hormonal?

Hormonal symptoms tend to cluster in recognizable patterns: fatigue paired with brain fog, sleep problems with mood changes, weight gain with muscle loss. Our free symptom screeners evaluate these patterns and tell you whether blood work is worth pursuing. Women use the Perimenopause Screener (validated 11-item Menopause Rating Scale plus a safety screen for hormone-therapy contraindications). Men use the Hormone Health Quiz (8 symptom categories matched to 10 archetypes). Neither is a diagnosis — they tell you whether testing is a reasonable next step.

What do the screeners measure?

The Perimenopause Screener uses the validated 11-item Menopause Rating Scale to evaluate hot flashes, sleep problems, mood, sexual and bladder symptoms, and physical changes, plus a safety screen for hormone-therapy contraindications. The men's Hormone Health Quiz evaluates symptoms across 8 categories — energy, mental clarity, mood, sleep, body composition, sexual health, physical symptoms, and recovery — and matches the pattern to one of 10 archetypes.

Are the screeners a medical diagnosis?

No. The screeners are educational tools that help identify symptom patterns consistent with hormonal imbalance. They are not a medical diagnosis. Blood work is the only way to confirm hormone levels and determine whether treatment is appropriate. The screeners help you decide whether testing is a reasonable next step.
