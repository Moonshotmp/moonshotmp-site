The real question isn't "what's cheapest?" — it's "what's included?" A $150/month program that doesn't include labs, DEXA scans, or proper monitoring isn't a better deal. It's a lower sticker price with unbundled costs.

HIDDEN COSTS TO WATCH FOR

TRT pricing can be opaque. Here are the most common ways clinics make a program look cheaper than it actually is:

Labs Billed Separately

This is the biggest one. Comprehensive hormone panels cost $200-500 per draw. If you're getting labs every 3 months (standard for the first year), that's $800-2,000/year in lab costs alone. Some clinics advertise a low monthly rate and then send you a separate lab bill. At Moonshot, labs are included — every 6 months, 60+ biomarkers, no separate bill.

Office Visit Fees

Some clinics charge $150-250 per follow-up visit, separate from your monthly program fee. If your dose needs adjustment — which is common, especially early on — each visit is an additional charge. At Moonshot, follow-up visits and dosing adjustments are included.

Pharmacy Markups

Some TRT programs use an in-house pharmacy with inflated prices or require you to fill prescriptions through a specific (more expensive) compounding pharmacy. Ask whether the testosterone cost is included in the monthly fee or billed separately through a pharmacy.

Membership or Platform Fees

Some telehealth platforms charge a monthly "membership fee" ($50-100/month) on top of the medication cost. You're paying for access to the platform and then paying again for the prescription. Read the fine print.

Ancillary Medications Not Included

Some men on TRT need ancillary medications like anastrozole (estrogen management) or HCG (fertility preservation). Some programs include these; others charge extra. Ask upfront whether ancillary medications are covered.

How to compare fairly: Ask every clinic this question: "What is my total out-of-pocket cost for the first 12 months, including all labs, visits, medications, and monitoring?" That number is the only honest comparison.

WHY CASH-PAY CAN BE CHEAPER THAN INSURANCE

This sounds counterintuitive. Insurance is supposed to make healthcare cheaper. For basic TRT — a prescription and minimal monitoring — it usually is. But for optimization-level TRT, the math often favors cash-pay.

Here's why: insurance covers treatment of a diagnosis. If your total testosterone is below 300 ng/dL, insurance will generally cover a testosterone prescription and a basic lab check once or twice a year. That's the floor of care.

What insurance typically doesn't cover:

Comprehensive Biomarker Panels

Insurance covers basic testosterone. Optimization requires tracking free testosterone, SHBG, estradiol, PSA, hematocrit, lipids, thyroid, metabolic markers — 60+ biomarkers. Most insurance plans won't cover this level of monitoring for "routine" TRT.

DEXA Body Composition Scans

Insurance covers DEXA for osteoporosis screening in specific populations. It does not cover DEXA for body composition tracking — which is the most objective way to measure whether TRT is actually changing your lean mass and fat distribution.

Dosing Optimization

Insurance-based TRT often means your PCP writes a standard dose, checks your level once a year, and calls it done. Optimization means dialing in your dose based on labs, symptoms, and response — with adjustments until you're in the right range for your body.

Provider Expertise

Most PCPs prescribe TRT but don't specialize in hormone optimization. They may not monitor estradiol, may not adjust protocols based on symptoms vs. just numbers, and may not be up to date on current optimization protocols. Specialization matters.

Then there's the insurance math itself. If you haven't met your deductible ($2,000-6,000 for many plans), you're paying out-of-pocket for labs and visits anyway — at insurance-negotiated rates that are often higher than cash-pay prices. A $400 lab panel through insurance (pre-deductible) might cost $285 cash-pay.

Cash-pay also eliminates prior authorizations, formulary restrictions, and the administrative friction of dealing with insurance companies. You get the medication your provider prescribes, at the dose they recommend, without a claims adjuster second-guessing the clinical decision.

To be clear: If you want basic TRT — a prescription and minimal monitoring — insurance is probably your cheapest option. If you want optimization-level care with comprehensive labs, DEXA tracking, and active dosing management, a cash-pay program like Moonshot's $235/month is often a better value than insurance + out-of-pocket monitoring costs.

IS TRT WORTH THE COST?

This is the real question. $235/month is $2,820/year. Is it worth it? That depends entirely on how much low testosterone is costing you right now — in energy, productivity, body composition, sleep quality, and quality of life.

Men with clinically low testosterone often report:

Energy & Productivity

Fatigue is the most common symptom of low testosterone. Men on optimized TRT consistently report significantly improved energy levels, mental clarity, and daily productivity. If low energy is costing you performance at work, the ROI calculation is straightforward.

Body Composition

Optimized testosterone levels support increased lean muscle mass and reduced body fat — especially when combined with strength training and proper nutrition. These changes are measurable via DEXA scan, which is why we include 2 scans per year in the program.

Sleep Quality

Low testosterone is associated with poor sleep quality and reduced deep sleep. Optimizing testosterone levels often improves sleep architecture, which cascades into better recovery, mood, and cognitive function.

Cognitive Function

Brain fog, difficulty concentrating, and reduced mental sharpness are common symptoms of low testosterone. Research shows that testosterone plays a role in cognitive function, and many men report improved focus and mental clarity on TRT.

The honest answer: TRT isn't for everyone. If your testosterone is in a healthy range and you feel fine, spending $235/month on optimization doesn't make sense. But if your levels are low and it's affecting your energy, body composition, sleep, and daily performance, the cost of not treating it is often higher than the cost of treatment.

The best way to find out is to get your levels checked. A comprehensive blood panel gives you data instead of guessing.

Worth noting: TRT is not a shortcut. It restores a hormonal foundation. You still need to train, eat well, sleep, and manage stress. What TRT does is remove the hormonal bottleneck that makes all of those things harder and less effective. Think of it as removing the governor on an engine — you still have to drive the car.

TRT COST FAQ

How much does TRT cost per month?

TRT costs vary widely depending on the provider model. At Moonshot Medical in Park Ridge, IL, the Men's Hormone Optimization program is $235 per month, all-inclusive. That covers medical oversight, testosterone, comprehensive labs every 6 months, 2 DEXA scans per year, 1 vitamin injection per month, and ongoing dosing adjustments. Telehealth clinics typically charge $150-300/month but often bill labs separately. Concierge clinics run $300-500/month.

Does insurance cover TRT?

Insurance can cover basic testosterone prescriptions if you meet diagnostic criteria (typically total testosterone below 300 ng/dL with symptoms). However, insurance-based TRT usually means limited monitoring — a basic testosterone level check once or twice a year, no DEXA scans, no comprehensive biomarker panels. You'll also deal with copays, deductibles, prior authorizations, and formulary restrictions. Many men find that a cash-pay optimization program provides better monitoring and outcomes than insurance-based TRT.

What's included in Moonshot's TRT program?

The $235/month Men's Hormone Optimization program includes: medical oversight and provider access, testosterone and supplies, comprehensive lab panels every 6 months (60+ biomarkers), 2 DEXA body composition scans per year, 1 vitamin injection per month, and ongoing dosing adjustments based on your labs and symptoms. Initial labs are a one-time cost of approximately $285.

Are there hidden fees?

At Moonshot Medical, no. The $235/month price includes labs, monitoring, medication, and DEXA scans. The only additional cost is initial labs (~$285, one-time). Many other clinics charge separately for labs ($200-500 per draw), office visits ($150-250 each), pharmacy fees, and membership fees on top of medication costs. Always ask what's included before committing to any TRT program.

Is TRT a lifetime commitment?

TRT is typically a long-term therapy, not a short course. Once you start exogenous testosterone, your body reduces its own production. If you stop, your natural levels will likely return to where they were before treatment — or temporarily lower while your body restarts production. Some men use TRT for years or decades. Others use it for a period and then taper off under medical supervision. Your provider will discuss the long-term implications before you start.

At other clinics, lab costs vary significantly: some charge $200-500 per blood draw on top of monthly fees, while insurance-based labs depend on your plan's coverage and deductible status. Lab costs are one of the most common hidden fees in TRT programs.
