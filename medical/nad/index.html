WHAT IS NAD+?

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme present in every living cell. It is not a supplement trend or a vitamin — it is required for the most fundamental metabolic processes that keep you alive.

NAD+ levels decline approximately 50% by age 60 (Massudi et al., 2012). This decline correlates directly with reduced energy, slower recovery, impaired cognitive function, and the metabolic dysfunction that characterizes aging.

The decline happens because CD38 — an enzyme that consumes NAD+ — increases with chronic inflammation as you age, while PARP enzymes consume more NAD+ to repair accumulating DNA damage. The result is a supply-demand mismatch that accelerates with every passing decade.

What NAD+ Does in Your Body:

  • • Mitochondrial energy production — drives ATP synthesis via the electron transport chain
  • • DNA repair — PARP enzymes consume NAD+ to fix single-strand breaks (tens of thousands per cell per day)
  • • Sirtuin activation — SIRT1-7 regulate aging, inflammation, and metabolism; all are NAD+-dependent
  • • Cellular signaling — calcium homeostasis, circadian rhythm regulation, immune function

WHO BENEFITS MOST FROM NAD+

NAD+ is not a magic bullet — but for the right candidates, the improvement in energy, recovery, and cognitive function is measurable.

Chronic Fatigue and Low Energy

If your mitochondria are starved for NAD+, ATP production drops. Patients with persistent fatigue that blood work has not explained often respond well to NAD+ therapy. It addresses the cellular energy deficit directly.

Slow Exercise Recovery

Athletes and active adults who notice recovery taking longer than it used to. NAD+ supports the repair pathways that clear metabolic waste and rebuild damaged tissue after training. Particularly popular with rehab and CrossFit athletes.

Brain Fog and Cognitive Decline

Neurons are among the most energy-demanding cells in your body. When NAD+ drops, cognitive function follows — poor focus, slower processing, memory lapses. Restoring NAD+ levels supports neuronal energy metabolism and sirtuin-mediated neuroprotection.

Adults 40+ With Age-Related Decline

NAD+ decline accelerates after 40. If you are noticing a general downward trend in energy, sleep quality, recovery, and vitality that does not have a specific diagnosis, depleted NAD+ is a physiological explanation worth addressing.

Hormone Therapy Patients

NAD+ supports the metabolic pathways that hormones activate. Patients on TRT or other hormone optimization protocols often see compounding benefits from adding NAD+ — the hormones signal your body to build and repair, and NAD+ provides the cellular fuel to execute.

Post-Illness Recovery

Viral infections and chronic illness deplete NAD+ reserves as your immune system and DNA repair machinery consume it at elevated rates. NAD+ therapy can help restore baseline cellular function during and after recovery.

What is the difference between SubQ injections and IV drips?

Both deliver NAD+ directly to the bloodstream. IV drips take 2-4 hours and cost $250-1,000+ due to infusion center overhead. Subcutaneous injections take 5 minutes, deliver clinical doses with near-complete bioavailability, and cost $60 at Moonshot. The molecule does not know how it got into your bloodstream.

How often should I get NAD+ injections?

A typical protocol starts with 1-2 injections per week for 2-4 weeks (loading phase), then transitions to weekly or biweekly maintenance. Some patients do monthly maintenance long-term. Your provider adjusts the schedule based on your goals and response.

Why not just take oral NMN or NR supplements?

Oral NAD+ precursors like NMN and NR have poor bioavailability — approximately 2-5% survives gut metabolism to reach your cells. Injectable NAD+ bypasses digestion entirely, delivering the full clinical dose to the bloodstream. For a deeper comparison, read our NAD+ vs NMN guide.

Can I combine NAD+ with TRT or peptide therapy?

Yes — and it is one of the most common combinations we see. NAD+ supports the metabolic pathways that hormones and peptides activate. Patients on testosterone optimization or peptide therapy often report compounding benefits from adding NAD+.

Is NAD+ therapy safe?

NAD+ injections are generally well-tolerated. The most common side effects are temporary flushing, warmth, or mild nausea — these resolve within minutes.
